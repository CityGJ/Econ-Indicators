Economic Commentary

Fed policy: first hike since 2023

On September 16, 2026 the FOMC voted 12–0 to raise the federal funds target range 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%, its first increase since July 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation has been “too high… for too long” and that the Committee needed more confidence inflation was moving toward its 2% goal before easing further. The post-meeting statement described activity as expanding at a solid pace, with job gains keeping pace with the workforce. The Committee's updated projections point toward a possible additional 25bp hike before year-end; the median participant sees the funds rate flat at 4.1% through 2027.

Source: Federal Reserve, FOMC statement & implementation note, Sept 16, 2026.

Leading indicators: growth cooling, not contracting

The Conference Board's Leading Economic Index edged down 0.1% in August 2026 — its first monthly decline since March — after a 0.2% rise in July. Four of ten components fell, led by weaker consumer expectations and a drop in building permits. The six-month growth rate turned slightly negative (−0.1%, Feb–Aug), reversing a brief positive stretch. The Conference Board's own read: “the economy is still expanding, but growth is expected to slow,” with its 2026 GDP forecast held at 1.9%.

Source: The Conference Board, US LEI release, Sept 18, 2026.

How this dashboard is refreshed

This dashboard refreshes automatically every Friday. A scheduled run pulls each series from its primary source (FRED series files for BLS, BEA, Census and EIA data; BLS and BEA releases for headline figures), ties every figure out against the release it came from, and republishes this page at the same address. No action is needed to keep it current, and the month stamped on each tile is that series' own latest release — different series sit on different release calendars.

ISM Manufacturing, ISM Services and the Conference Board Leading Economic Index are licensed and are not redistributed on FRED, so they are not pulled from a data file. Each issuer does publish the headline figure in its own press release (ISM Report On Business; The Conference Board US LEI release), and the weekly run takes the figure from that release, cites it in the provenance log, and writes nothing that the release does not state.

Use “Copy refresh request” only for an off-cycle pull — a figure that moved between Fridays, or a series the last run could not source. It copies a prompt naming exactly which series are stale and as of when; paste it into Claude to trigger a run outside the schedule.

Update this dashboard with new data

Use this for the corrections to any figure, or to enter an ISM or Conference Board LEI headline before the Friday run picks it up from the issuer’s release. Paste tab-separated rows in the source workbook's column order: Date, GDP QoQ, NFP, Unemployment, Job Openings, Claims 4W Avg, Cont Claims, CPI MoM, CPI YoY, Core CPI MoM, Core CPI YoY, PCE MoM, PCE YoY, Core PCE MoM, Core PCE YoY, AHE YoY, FHFA YoY, ISM Services, ISM Mfg, Existing Homes, Auto Sales, Retail Sales YoY, LEI MoM, Gas Price, Credit Spreads. Leave a cell empty to leave that figure alone. A row for a month that already exists replaces it; new months are added.

Scope of an entry made here: it is held in this browser only — nothing is uploaded, and no one else sees it. The next Friday refresh replaces it. To make an entry permanent, send it to IT so it goes into the published page.
